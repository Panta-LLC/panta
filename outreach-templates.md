# Week 3 Outreach Templates
**Status:** Working draft v0.1 · Goal: 5 discovery calls booked from ~30 touches

**The math to keep in mind:** warm referral asks convert at roughly 10–20% to a conversation; personalized "I noticed" outreach at 5–10%; generic cold email at 1–2%. So the sequencing is: exhaust warm first, then personalized local, and never send generic. Every template below has a personalization slot — if you can't fill it, don't send it.

**Voice rules for all templates:** short enough to read on a phone. One ask per message. No "I hope this finds you well." Never attach anything to a first touch. The word "audit" only appears after the problem does.

---

## 1. The Warm Network Ask (your primary Week 3 play)

*To: friends, former colleagues, family — people who like you but aren't prospects themselves. This is a referral ask, not a pitch. Send 20–30 of these, individually personalized, never BCC'd.*

**Subject:** quick favor — know anyone like this?

> Hey [Name],
>
> [One genuine personal line — reference something real: their kid's season, their job change, the last time you talked.]
>
> Quick favor: I've launched a consulting practice helping small businesses and community organizations fix their online presence — the stuff that quietly costs them customers, like a contact form that fails on phones or a Google listing with the wrong hours.
>
> Who's one person you know that runs a small business, nonprofit, or practice and complains about getting found online? I'm taking three founding clients at a reduced rate in exchange for honest feedback, so this is a genuinely good moment to know me.
>
> No pressure to broker anything — a name and "mind if I mention you?" is plenty.
>
> [You]

**Why it works:** "Who's one person" beats "know anyone?" — a specific question retrieves a specific memory. The founding-client frame turns the referral from a favor *they're* doing into a deal *their friend* gets.

## 2. Warm Direct (a contact who IS a fit)

**Subject:** something I noticed about [Business Name]

> Hey [Name],
>
> [Personal line.]
>
> I've started a practice helping [businesses like yours / organizations like [Org]] with exactly the thing I noticed when I looked you up last week: [ONE specific, true observation — "your Yelp listing still shows the Maple St. address" / "your Instagram links to a dead Linktree" / "searching '[their service] [city]' brings up two competitors before you"].
>
> That's usually the visible tip of a few fixable leaks. I'm doing a full diagnostic — every place you exist online, where the gaps are, what they cost, and a prioritized fix list — for three founding clients at $750 (goes to $950 after).
>
> Worth a 20-minute call? Even if it's a no, I'll tell you how to fix the [observation] for free — it's a 15-minute job.
>
> [You]

**The rule:** the observation must be real, specific, and *small*. You're demonstrating the method, not diagnosing their whole business in an email. The free-fix closer costs you nothing and makes the no-risk claim credible.

## 3. Local Cold-Warm ("I noticed") — small business

*For businesses you've actually scanned. 10 minutes of research per send: check their GBP, run their site through PageSpeed on your phone, try their contact form. Send only where you found something.*

**Subject:** your contact form (quick heads up)

> Hi [Name],
>
> I was looking at [Business] — [one true connection or compliment: "my neighbor recommended you" / "your before-and-afters on Instagram are genuinely impressive"] — and noticed something you'd want to know: [the finding, stated as a heads-up, not a pitch — "your contact form's submit button doesn't work on iPhones — the cookie banner covers it"].
>
> I run a practice that finds exactly this kind of leak for local businesses. No pitch needed on that one though — [one-line fix].
>
> If you're curious what else a full scan turns up, happy to show you — I map every place a business exists online and grade what's working. Takes 20 minutes to walk through.
>
> Either way, hope the form fix helps.
>
> [You]

**Note:** you *give away* the finding. Counterintuitive and correct — the reciprocity plus demonstrated competence is what earns the call, and one finding was never going to be worth $950 anyway.

## 4. Nonprofit Variant (the Ad Grant hook)

**Subject:** is [Org] using its $10k/month from Google?

> Hi [Name],
>
> [Genuine line about their mission or a recent program — from their site or newsletter, specific enough to prove you read it.]
>
> Quick question that's worth real money: is [Org] using the Google Ad Grant? It's $10,000/month in free search advertising for nonprofits — and most either haven't claimed it or have it misconfigured so it's spending a fraction of that. [If checkable: "I took a quick look and couldn't find your ads running for searches like '[relevant query]', which usually means it's unclaimed or stalled."]
>
> I help community organizations fix their whole online presence — how donors, volunteers, and funders find and vet you — and the Ad Grant check is part of a diagnostic I'm offering three founding organizations at $640 (15% nonprofit rate on the founding price).
>
> Worth 20 minutes? If the Grant's the only issue, I'll point you to the application and you're welcome.
>
> [You]

## 5. Practice Variant (therapists, clinicians, advisors)

**Subject:** how new clients find [Practice Name]

>

>
> I work with practices on how new clients actually find and choose them — which for most clinicians happens on directories and Google before your website ever loads. When I looked up [Practice], I noticed [one true finding: "your Psychology Today profile doesn't mention you're accepting new clients" / "your Google listing's booking link goes to a page that's down"].
>
> I do a full diagnostic of every channel — directories, Google, insurance panel listings, your site, and the inquiry-to-booking path — built around what your ethics code actually allows (no testimonial-soliciting advice you can't use). $750 for founding clients, and everything in the report is yours whether we ever work together again.
>
> Open to 20 minutes?
>
> [You]

## 6. Community Institution Intro (chamber, business association, nonprofit network)

*Not a pitch for clients — a pitch to be useful to the institution. The clients come from the relationship.*

**Subject:** free workshop offer for [Chamber/Network] members

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm [You], a [Davis/Sacramento]-based consultant helping small businesses and community organizations fix their online presence — I come from [X] years in software engineering, so I handle both the strategy and the technical side.
>
> I'd love to offer your members a free 45-minute workshop: "The 5 Places Your Business Is Leaking Customers Online" — practical, no pitch, they leave with a self-check list. I can also do free 10-minute presence scans at any member event.
>
> Is there a slot in an upcoming meeting or newsletter where this would be useful? Happy to work around your calendar.
>
> [You]

**Why this shape:** institutions need programming more than members need another vendor. One workshop = 20 warm prospects who've watched you demonstrate the method live.

## 7. Follow-Up Sequence (for templates 2–5)

**Bump 1 — 4 days later, same thread:**
> Hi [Name] — floating this up. The short version: [restate the specific finding in one line]. Worth 20 minutes?

**Bump 2 — 5 days after that, same thread, add value:**
> Last nudge from me. Whether or not we talk, here's the fix for the [finding]: [2–3 line instruction or a link]. If the rest ever feels worth mapping, you know where I am.

Then stop. Two bumps, always in-thread, never guilt ("I know you're busy…" reads as pressure). The value-add in bump 2 converts a surprising number of dead threads weeks later.

## 8. Launch Announcement (LinkedIn / social)

*Post once the site is live. Personal narrative beats corporate announcement.*

> After [X] years building software for [types of companies], I kept noticing the same thing about the small businesses and community organizations around me: they're excellent at what they do and invisible — or worse, broken — where people look for them.
>
> A landscaper with 47 five-star reviews and a contact form that fails on phones. A nonprofit leaving $10k/month of free Google advertising unclaimed. A therapist whose booking link 404s.
>
> So that's the practice I've launched: I map every place an organization exists online, find where it's leaking customers or donors, and hand over a prioritized fix list — then build whatever needs building. Strategy and engineering, one person.
>
> First offer is a Digital Presence Audit: 10 business days, flat price, and a third of the roadmap is stuff you can do yourself for free. Three founding-client spots at a reduced rate.
>
> If that's you — or someone you know — link in comments. And if you just want the self-check version, comment "checklist" and I'll send you the 5 things to look at yourself.

**The "comment checklist" mechanic** creates engagement, surfaces warm leads, and gives you a reason to DM people. Have the checklist ready before posting (it's the DIY items from your rubric, reformatted — 30 minutes of work).

## Tracking (keep it stupid-simple)

One spreadsheet, five columns: Name · Segment · Template used · Date of last touch · Status (sent / bumped / call booked / audit / dead). Review every Friday. The only metrics that matter in Week 3: touches sent, calls booked, and which template each call came from — that last one tells you where to double down in Week 4.

## Sequencing for the week

- **Mon–Tue:** all warm network asks (template 1) — momentum from easy sends
- **Tue–Wed:** warm directs (2) + LinkedIn launch post (8)
- **Wed–Thu:** 5–8 researched local sends (3–5) — quality over volume
- **Thu–Fri:** 2–3 institution intros (6)
- **Daily:** bumps per the follow-up schedule; respond to everything within 4 hours — *you're selling response speed, so model it*
