Three scripts for the Panta website: a founder story, an opportunity/positioning piece, and a service explainer for the Web Presence Assessment. Each is written for direct-to-camera delivery with b-roll cutaways, and each ends on a single clear CTA.

**General delivery notes:**
- Conversational pace, ~140 words/minute. Pauses marked with (beat).
- On-screen text (OST) reinforces key phrases — never duplicates full sentences.
- Suggested runtime is per-script; trim b-roll, not spoken lines, if you're over.

## Script 1 — "Two Worlds" (Founder Story / Mission)

**Target runtime:** 90 seconds
**Placement:** About page hero, LinkedIn launch post, YouTube channel intro
**Tone:** Personal, reflective opening → confident, mission-driven close

### COLD OPEN — 0:00–0:10

**[Direct to camera, tight shot]**

> I've spent fifteen years of my career split between two worlds.

**(beat)**

> On one side: multimillion-dollar agencies and venture-funded startups. On the other: bootstrapped small businesses and community organizations running on almost nothing.

*OST: "Two worlds. Fifteen years."*
*B-roll: split-screen — polished office/product shots vs. community org in action (DBI photography works here)*

### THE INSIGHT — 0:10–0:35

> Here's what surprised me. The problems were the same in both worlds. **(beat)** Reaching the right people. Building trust. Turning attention into action.

> What was different — the *only* thing that was different — was the resources available to solve them.

> The funded teams could throw money at the problem. The small teams couldn't. For them, strategy, partnership, and creativity weren't nice-to-haves. They were the difference between an operation that worked and one that struggled to survive.

*OST: "Same problems. Different resources."*
*B-roll: hands-on work — whiteboard, laptop, site build in progress*

### THE MISSION — 0:35–1:05

> That's why I started Panta.

> We work with small businesses and community organizations to close that gap. Strategic planning. Technical direction. Creative production. **(beat)** The same caliber of thinking the big-budget teams get — scaled to what you actually have.

> Because the truth is, most small organizations don't need more resources to make a bigger impact. They need a clearer picture of what's possible, and a partner who can help them execute within their means.

*OST: "Understand what's possible. Execute within your means."*
*B-roll: client collaboration moments, before/after site reveals (AHT, DBI case studies)*

### CLOSE — 1:05–1:30

**[Back to tight direct-to-camera]**

> If you're building something that matters and doing it without a big budget — that's exactly who we built Panta for.

> Let's talk about what's possible.

*OST: panta.llc — Schedule a free consultation*
*End card: Panta ripple mark, cream background*

## Script 4 — "The Short Version" (Homepage Hero Intro)

**Target runtime:** 50 seconds
**Placement:** Homepage hero, right-hand column — the slot currently holding the
Delta Bay Impact testimonial card
**Tone:** Warm, direct, unhurried. A handshake, not a pitch — the viewer has been
on the page for four seconds and hasn't decided anything yet.

**What this script has to do that the others don't:** the hero copy beside it
already states the offer and the problem. This video's only job is to put a
person behind the company and establish competence fast. Do not restate the
headline — the page says it louder, and hearing it twice reads as thin.

**Silent-first:** assume it plays muted until someone chooses otherwise. Every
beat needs to land on OST alone.

## Production Notes (all four)

- **Visual system:** Cream backgrounds, charcoal text, bamboo green accents for OST highlights. Spectral for headline OST, Instrument Sans for supporting text — keep the video type consistent with the site.
- **Shooting order suggestion:** Shoot all three direct-to-camera segments in one session (consistent wardrobe/lighting), then gather b-roll separately. The DBI and AHT projects are your b-roll library.
- **Cutdowns:** Each script's hook + CTA works as a standalone 15-second short for Instagram/LinkedIn. Script 3's hook is the strongest performer candidate for paid retargeting.
- **CTA consistency:** All four funnel to the free consultation / assessment booking. One destination, one action. Script 4 is the exception that proves it: it names the offer but shows no button, because the page it sits on already has one.
- **Shoot Script 4 in the same session.** It shares wardrobe, lighting, and the "same problems, different budgets" line with Script 1 — but it is NOT a trim of Script 1. Script 1 is a 90-second story for a page someone chose to visit; Script 4 has five seconds to earn attention from someone who has not chosen anything yet.

### Script 4 delivery constraints (homepage hero)

- **Aspect ratio:** the hero's right-hand column is roughly square at desktop and
  full-width at mobile. Frame for a **1:1 or 4:5 crop** — a 16:9 master will
  letterbox into a thin strip in that column and waste the height. Shoot wider
  than you need and crop.
- **Captions burned in**, not just a track. Silent autoplay is the default state,
  and burned-in captions survive every platform the cutdown gets reposted to.
- **File weight:** this loads in the first viewport of the site's most-visited
  page. Keep the hero cut under ~5 MB, serve a poster image so nothing shifts
  while it loads, and never autoplay with sound.
- **Do not autoplay-and-loop.** A looping founder video beside a call-to-action
  competes with the thing it is supposed to support — the same mistake the hero
  slideshow made.
