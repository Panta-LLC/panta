# Digital Presence Blueprint — Service Definition
**Status:** Working draft v0.1 · **Last updated:** July 24, 2026 · Companion to the Digital Presence Audit service definition

---

## 1. Overview & Positioning

The Digital Presence Blueprint is the second front door to the same offering as the Audit. One standard — the 40-check Presence Scorecard — applied in the other direction:

- **Audit** (existing presence): grade what exists against the standard → gap list → fix roadmap
- **Blueprint** (starting fresh): hand over the standard as a sequenced build plan → launch roadmap

**The core premise is unchanged:** customers, clients, donors, and funders need to **find** you, **trust** you, and **choose** you. The Blueprint designs that arc from day one instead of repairing it later.

**The promise:** In 10 business days, you'll have a complete, prioritized plan for your entire digital presence — which channels matter for your audience, in what order to claim and build them, and what "done right" looks like for each — with every step tagged as something you can do yourself or something worth hiring out.

**The pitch to the skeptic ("I have nothing to audit"):** You almost certainly already exist online — an auto-generated Google Business Profile, a Yelp page someone else seeded, a directory that scraped your registration. The Blueprint starts with a light scan that finds these fragments. *"You already exist online — you're just not in control of it yet."*

**Positioning line for the pair:** *Have a presence? We'll find the leaks. Starting fresh? We'll hand you the blueprint.* One standard, two ways in.

## 2. Who It's For

Same three segments as the Audit (nonprofits, professional services, e-commerce/small business), at a different life stage:

- **Pre-launch or newly launched** — registered the business, maybe bought a domain, little or nothing built
- **The invisible operating business** — running for years on word of mouth; online presence is fragments they don't control
- **Rebrand / renames / relocations** — the old presence is wrong enough that starting from the standard beats patching (the discovery call decides Audit vs. Blueprint here)

**Routing rule (spectrum, not binary):** Intake Q17 ("list every place your organization exists online") plus the fragment scan determines the fit. Rough guide: if fewer than ~10 of the 40 scorecard checks could even be graded, it's a Blueprint. The discovery call makes the final call — the client never has to self-diagnose.

## 3. What's Included

### 3a. Fragment Scan
What already exists that you didn't create or don't control: auto-generated GBP, directory scrapes, review-site stubs, name collisions/confusables in search. Every fragment is either **claim**, **correct**, or **suppress** on the roadmap. Also checks name availability across the channels that matter (handles, domain variants) before you commit to them.

### 3b. Entry Point Plan
The Audit's Entry Point Map, forward-looking: which channels your specific audience actually uses, which you should build now, which to deliberately skip (a dormant profile is worse than none — freshness is a trust signal), and how they'll link to each other (the hub-and-spoke, designed instead of diagnosed).

### 3c. The Standard, as a Build Checklist
The full 40-check scorecard reframed as targets: what "2 points" looks like for every check in Visibility, Trust, Conversion, and Systems — so every build decision has a definition of done. Segment variants apply (Candid/GuideStar for nonprofits, industry directories for practices, Merchant Center for e-commerce).

### 3d. Top 3 Launch Priorities
The Blueprint's version of Money Leaks: the three places a new presence leaks first, for your segment and audience — stated in plain language with the cost of skipping them. (Typical examples: an unclaimed GBP while a confusable competitor ranks; no measurement from day one; a booking path that dead-ends on mobile.)

### 3e. Day-One Measurement Setup
GA4 + Search Console wired before launch, conversion events defined with the client's actual goals. The structural advantage over almost every established competitor: **never flying blind, from the first visitor.** This becomes the baseline the free 90-day re-score measures against.

### 3f. Conversion Path Design
The primary audience's path from first touch to conversion, designed on paper before anything is built: the CTA on every channel, the contact/booking flow in ≤3 steps, the response-speed system. Multi-audience segments (donor/volunteer/participant) each get a path.

### 3g. Competitive Context
Same as the Audit: 2–3 comparable organizations, what they do across the channel map, and where a new entrant can beat them quickly (usually: freshness, response speed, and measurement).

## 4. Deliverables

1. **Presence Standard Checklist** — the 40-check scorecard as build targets, segment-adapted
2. **Entry Point Plan** — the channel map, designed: build now / build later / skip, with the cross-link scheme
3. **Top 3 Launch Priorities** — plain-language, cost-of-skipping stated
4. **90-Day Launch Sequence** — every step ordered and tagged **[DIY]** (with guide) or **[Project]** (worth hiring out)
5. **Fragment Scan Report** — what already exists, with claim/correct/suppress actions
6. **Blueprint Walkthrough** — 60-minute live presentation (this is where build projects are scoped)

> **The honesty mechanic carries over:** roughly a third of the launch sequence should be genuinely free DIY — claiming profiles, setting up analytics from guides, writing the GBP description. Same credibility engine as the Audit.

## 5. Process & Timeline (10 business days)

| Day | Step |
|-----|------|
| 0 | Intake questionnaire sent (same instrument; Q17 + fragment scan route to Blueprint) |
| 1–2 | 45-min discovery call — goals, audiences, segment, name/brand constraints |
| 3–6 | Fragment scan + channel research + standard adaptation + conversion path design |
| 7–9 | Blueprint assembly |
| 10 | 60-min walkthrough + written blueprint delivered |

## 6. Pricing

| Offer | Price |
|-------|-------|
| Digital Presence Blueprint | **$750** |
| Founding-client rate (first 3) | $600 |
| Nonprofit rate | 15% off published pricing |

**Rationale:** priced under the Audit ($950) — less scanning labor, lower barrier for the newest businesses, and Blueprint clients have the highest downstream value (they need everything built). Downstream offers are identical to the Audit's: Brand Foundation $3.5–5.5k · Web Presence Build $5–9k · Custom Tool Build $4–12k · Growth Retainer $850–1,800/mo. The Web Presence Build is the natural next step for most Blueprint clients.

**Free 90-day re-score** applies here too: after launch, the Blueprint client gets scored against the standard for the first time — the "we went from invisible to a B in ninety days" story is the testimonial engine.

## 7. Tooling

Same stack as the Audit (see its §7). Notes:

- The **Presence Scanner** runs in *fragment mode* for Blueprint intakes: business name + city (no URL needed) → GBP/Places lookup, directory & review-site stub search, name-collision check. This doubles as the pre-discovery sales demo for skeptics.
- **BrightLocal citation builder** does the bulk directory claiming during the build phase — a natural [Project] item.
- The **Cross-Link Checker** validates the hub-and-spoke *after* build — it's the 90-day re-score workhorse for Blueprint clients.

## 8. Relationship to the Audit (routing & sales)

- **One intake, one discovery call, two doors.** Never make the prospect choose the product; the call does it.
- **Gray zone default:** if an org has a real-but-broken presence (rebrand, relocation, years-dormant), default to the **Audit** — finding what's wrong with what exists is the persuasive artifact, and the roadmap can still say "rebuild from the standard."
- **Website copy fork:** *"Have a presence? We'll find the leaks. Starting fresh? We'll hand you the blueprint."*
- **Proof case:** Panta itself is the first Blueprint client — this site was built to the standard before any client saw it.
