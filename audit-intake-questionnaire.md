# Digital Presence Audit — Client Intake Questionnaire
**Status:** Working draft v0.1 · Sent at Day 0, due before the discovery call

*Client-facing intro:* This takes about 20 minutes and makes your audit dramatically more useful — your answers tell us what "success" means for you, so we audit against your goals rather than generic best practices. Skip anything you don't know; "I don't know" is itself useful information.

## Section 2: Goals & Success

8. What made you decide to get this audit *now*? (What's the itch?)
9. If this engagement works perfectly, what's different 6 months from now? Be specific — a number if you have one (clients/month, revenue, donations, bookings).
10. What's your single most important "conversion"? (e.g., booked consult, online sale, donation, membership signup, phone call)
11. What's a new customer/client/donor worth to you, roughly? (First transaction and lifetime, if you know it)
12. Rank what matters most right now (1 = most): ___ Being found by new people · ___ Converting the people who already find you · ___ Looking more credible/professional · ___ Understanding what's working (measurement)

## Section 3: Your Audience

13. Describe your ideal customer/client/donor. Who are they, and what's going on in their life when they need you?
14. Do you serve more than one distinct audience? (e.g., donors AND volunteers; parents AND referring physicians) List each.
15. How do people *currently* find you, as far as you know? Rank your guesses: word of mouth / Google / social / directories / referrals / other.
16. When someone is deciding between you and an alternative, what tips them your way?

## Section 4: Current Presence

17. List every place your organization exists online (we'll find the rest — this tells us what YOU know about):
    - Website(s):
    - Google Business Profile: ☐ Yes, I manage it ☐ Exists but not sure who controls it ☐ Not sure ☐ No
    - Social accounts (list with handles):
    - Directory listings you know of:
    - Review sites where you appear:
    - Email newsletter: ☐ Yes (tool: ___, list size: ___) ☐ No
18. Who built your current website, and when was it last meaningfully updated?
19. Can you (or someone on your team) edit the website today? ☐ Yes, easily ☐ Yes, painfully ☐ No
20. Is there anything online about your organization that's *wrong* right now? (Old address, former staff, dead pages, an account you lost access to)

## Section 5: Past & Present Efforts

21. What have you already tried to improve your online presence? What happened?
22. Are you paying for any marketing/tools right now? (Ads, SEO service, social manager, website subscriptions, etc.) List with rough monthly cost.
23. Any past vendor relationships we should know about? (Especially: anyone who still controls your domain, website, or accounts)
24. How do you currently ask for reviews or testimonials, if at all?

## Section 6: Competitive Context

25. Name 2–3 organizations your audience might choose instead of you (local competitors, similar nonprofits, alternative solutions).
26. Anyone whose online presence you admire — in your field or outside it?

## Section 7: Working Together

27. Who is the decision-maker for acting on audit recommendations?
28. Realistically, how much time per week could someone on your team spend on [DIY] roadmap items? ☐ <1 hr ☐ 1–3 hrs ☐ 3+ hrs ☐ None — we'd want it done for us
29. Rough budget expectations for fixes beyond the audit, if any come up? (No commitment — this helps us tag the roadmap realistically) ☐ <$1k ☐ $1–5k ☐ $5–15k ☐ Depends on the case ☐ Prefer not to say
30. Anything you're worried we'll find? 🙂

## Internal Notes (not client-facing)

- Q9 + Q11 give you the ROI math for the findings presentation ("this leak costs roughly $X/month").
- Q12 + Q28 + Q29 calibrate the DIY/Project tagging before you write the roadmap.
- Q15 vs. actual analytics = the "perception gap" moment in the presentation — often the most persuasive slide.
- Q20, Q23, and the access checklist surface ownership risks early; these frequently become urgent [Project] items.
- Q30 builds rapport and often surfaces the real anxiety driving the purchase.
